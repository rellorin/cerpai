AI in cERP — ranked opportunities and paid-feature strategy

Review draft for the AI IN Cerp meeting — Monday, October 19, 2026
Prepared October 18, 2026. This is a product and business-process proposal for discussion, not an implementation commitment.

Recommended direction

cERP has a strong foundation for AI because it already links contract material, assignments/openings, releases, purchase orders, shop/work orders, inventory, and billing. The biggest opportunity is to help users interpret incoming information and understand what those linked records mean for their next decision.

My highest-impact investment would be submittal and revision assistance. My recommended first production pilot is release-readiness assistance, because it can deliver a useful, read-only result using existing structured data. A focused pilot should answer: “What is preventing this release from being ready, which records prove it, and what should I do next?”

Use the same underlying cERP services for page actions, scheduled summaries, and a future chatbot. Keep quantities, prices, status calculations, compatibility rules, and posting in validated application logic. AI should interpret, explain, suggest, and prepare drafts around that logic.

For commercial packaging, sell business capabilities with a usage allowance, rather than access to a particular model. Use Feature Management/Activation for administrator control, but enforce paid entitlement and consumption limits in the execution service as well.

About this review

This report is based on a targeted review of cERP workflows covering contracts, document intake, release schedules, purchasing, quoting, billing, inventory, configuration, field service, work orders, and scheduling. The opportunities are product hypotheses for discussion and should be validated with representative customer processes, data, and measurable outcomes before implementation.

Ranking is an analyst judgment, based on likely labor saved, financial and operational consequence, frequency, and breadth of users affected. Impact order is separate from suggested build order. Document-heavy contractors may favor submittal assistance; distribution-heavy customers may favor purchasing and inventory; service-heavy customers may move field closeout higher.

Difficulty scale: 1 = low; 2 = low–medium; 3 = medium; 4 = high; 5 = very high. Ratings assume a shared identity, entitlement, metering, and AI integration foundation exists.

Detailed opportunities and meeting notes

1. Submittal intake and revision-impact assistant

What users would experience. An estimator or project manager brings in a submittal, material schedule, or revision. cERP proposes item/vendor matches, flags missing attributes and ambiguous openings, and presents an understandable change list: added, removed, changed, and already committed. Each proposed value links back to its source page or supplied file.

Example to explain in the meeting: “A revised schedule changes the finish on twelve openings. Show me which are still open, which have already been ordered, and which need a change-order or supplier conversation.” This example is illustrative, not an observed customer incident.

Why it ranks first. Data entry is only part of the cost. A missed revision can propagate into purchasing, fabrication, delivery, and billing. Assistance at the point of intake can prevent downstream rework across several departments.

Existing foundation. CERP Import Management accepts staged contract/material data, resolves cross-references, records import exceptions, and checks whether material can be replaced. CERP Submittal Sync Check already compares vendor, description, item/category/group, quote reference, expiry, and attributes. The tests cover initial import and replacement of open material on reimport.

AI contribution and integration. Use document extraction for PDFs/scans, a Foundry-hosted language model to interpret descriptions and propose mappings, and existing AL logic to identify actual differences and replacement eligibility. Retain the existing XML path for structured inputs. An especially practical first increment is an explanation of existing revision differences and import exceptions; broader PDF intake follows.

Difficulty: 4/5. The hard work is stable line/opening identity, inconsistent supplier descriptions, dimensions/hand/finish, and revisions that affect committed material. Arbitrary drawing interpretation should be a later, separately evaluated scope.

Boundaries and measurement. Require review before import/change application; never let the model override ReplaceableOnImport or silently delete committed lines. Recheck the current record before applying a reviewed proposal. Measure review minutes per revision, accepted match rate, unresolved exceptions, and corrections after import. Proposed feature: AI.SubmittalReview; meter by bounded document/revision job.

Presenter note: “We already know how to import and protect material. AI helps users understand messy incoming information and the consequences of a revision.”

2. Release-readiness and exception assistant

What users would experience. On Release Schedule Status, an Explain readiness action returns a short list of blockers, responsible parties where recorded, and linked records. A scheduled version prepares a project manager’s daily list of releases requiring attention, with duplicate alerts suppressed.

Example: “This release is due Friday. One component is promised Monday, one work order remains incomplete, and approval to order is missing on another line. Here are the records to review.” Missing dates should be reported as unknown, not treated as on-time delivery.

Value. Project managers spend less time reconstructing the story across purchasing, inventory, production, and delivery. Earlier intervention can reduce expediting, incomplete shipments, and idle installers.

Existing foundation. Release Schedule contains due dates, approval, deposit requirements, delivery scheduling references, status, location, and latest PO promised receipt date. Its Status() procedure evaluates material and assignments. The status page exposes navigation, line details, stock association, and release/service actions. Lifecycle tests cover purchase, receipt, shop-order, sale, and shipping transitions.

AI contribution and integration. Create a deterministic readiness service that returns blockers and supporting record IDs. Let AI explain the result and draft a follow-up. Existing alerts and ordinary filters can identify overdue records without a model; AI adds cross-document explanation and communication. Use Foundry through an embedded page experience, with scheduled summaries later.

Difficulty: 3/5. The information is largely structured, but joins must distinguish material ready-to-ship from overall release state, and handle partial quantities and assignments correctly. Do not build around the obsolete CriticalReleaseCount procedure.

Boundaries and measurement. Pilot read-only. Do not autonomously release, reschedule, or waive deposits/approvals. Show the data timestamp. Measure time to diagnose a release, actionable-alert rate, and blockers found before due dates. Proposed feature: AI.ReleaseReadiness; interactive allowance plus a separate scheduled-run allowance.

Presenter note: “The board tells us status. This explains why, shows the evidence, and prepares the next step.”

3. Vendor acknowledgment and purchasing follow-up

What users would experience. A purchaser uploads or selects a vendor acknowledgment. cERP compares it with the PO and highlights changed promised dates, quantities, costs, substitutions, and vendor references. It drafts the proposed field updates and a supplier follow-up message.

Value. Acknowledgments and follow-up are repetitive, while a small date or specification change can disrupt an entire release. This also keeps release-readiness information more current.

Existing foundation. Purchase-status pages and purchase-management routines operate on purchase documents; cERP associations connect purchases back to contract/release/material records. Receiving tests explicitly cover Vendor Order No., Promised Receipt Date, Direct Unit Cost, staging, partial receipts, and receipt-only posting.

AI contribution and integration. Document extraction and a language model interpret supplier wording; deterministic matching reconciles PO/line/item/UOM and calculates differences. Start with upload on a PO to avoid mailbox integration being a prerequisite. Copilot Studio/Power Automate could later supply a monitored-mailbox intake channel, subject to explicit mailbox setup.

Difficulty: 4/5. Suppliers split and combine lines, use alternate item references, and distinguish ship dates from receipt dates. Currency, pack size, UOM, and ambiguous matches need explicit handling.

Boundaries and measurement. Purchaser confirms material changes. Receiving remains based on what physically arrived; an acknowledgment must never post a receipt. Draft messages require review initially. Detect duplicate documents and record changes since comparison. Measure minutes per acknowledgment, matching corrections, and time from supplier change to PO update. Proposed feature: AI.Purchasing; meter acknowledged-document jobs, with background intake separately enabled.

Presenter note: “AI reads the vendor’s response, shows exactly where it differs from our PO, and helps the purchaser react.”

4. Contract margin and cost-risk assistant

What users would experience. From contract statistics, users ask “What is driving the difference between planned and current cost?” The response identifies the largest supported variances across material, work orders, labor, and expenses, with record links and a suggested review order.

Value. Users can act on cost erosion before the contract closes, and managers need less manual analysis to prepare a project review.

Existing foundation. Contract Statistics Management exposes released/shipped/invoiced profit measures and actual/estimated/projected remaining costs. Contract Billing Summary exposes planned and actual material/work-order/task costs, invoiced amounts, and an under/over-billed measure.

AI contribution and integration. AL calculates and reconciles numbers. AI turns a structured variance breakdown into an explanation, clearly separating a recorded driver from a possible cause requiring investigation. The first version needs no predictive model. A later forecast needs reliable historical snapshots and outcome data; current-state fields alone do not establish a training dataset.

Difficulty: 3/5 for explanation; 4/5 for forecasting. Cost recognition, committed versus posted costs, currency, and change-order scope must be consistent. Avoid double-counting a cost across order and posted views.

Boundaries and measurement. Restrict financial information to authorized users. No automatic price changes or accounting entries. Measure review preparation time, confirmed actionable variances, and action taken; do not count every flagged variance as recovered margin. Proposed feature: AI.ContractInsights; meter contract analyses and scheduled portfolio runs separately.

Presenter note: “We already calculate costs. AI helps a manager understand where to investigate and explains the numbers in plain language.”

5. RFQ and quote preparation assistant

What users would experience. An estimator compares vendor offers against requested scope, sees exclusions and missing information, and receives a draft customer quote or clarification request. Similar historical work can be retrieved as a reference, subject to customer and company boundaries.

Value. Faster quote turnaround and more complete bid comparisons. Hidden exclusions and expired negotiated pricing can matter more than the lowest headline price.

Existing foundation. RFQ generates a vendor-selection worksheet. Stored Vendor Quotes tracks negotiated cost, validity, quantities, and consumption. Vendor Quote Management filters quotes by vendor/item/date and available quantity. Project Quote and the test suite support pricing overrides, price locking, and conversion references.

AI contribution and integration. Interpret offers and exclusions with a model; use cERP pricing and quote-eligibility routines for price calculations. Present a comparison with “not provided” for unknown freight, lead time, or terms. Start with a few vendor formats and an existing material list.

Difficulty: 4/5. Scope equivalence, dimensional pricing, packages, quantity limits, and customer-specific terms make automatic quote creation harder than document summarization.

Boundaries and measurement. Estimator selects the supplier and approves customer pricing. No AI-generated guarantee of technical equivalence or award probability. Measure quote preparation time, clarification rounds, and errors caught before submission. Proposed feature: AI.Quoting; meter a bounded quote/comparison job.

Presenter note: “It prepares the comparison and draft; our pricing engine and estimator still decide the commercial offer.”

6. Billing-readiness and revenue-recovery assistant

What users would experience. Billing staff receive a queue of completed or partially completed work that may need billing review, with supporting shipments, work/time records, stored-material amounts, and change-order context. AI prepares an explanation and a checklist of missing evidence for the payment application.

Value. Less time assembling billing support, fewer incomplete submissions, and earlier investigation of potentially missed charges.

Existing foundation. AIA Billing, Lump Sum Billing Management, Contract Billing Summary, and billing-line cost/revenue factboxes provide specialized billing structures. The billing buffer includes shipped/received quantities and planned/order/WIP/actual cost fields.

AI contribution and integration. Deterministic rules find candidate exceptions. AI explains them and assembles a referenced narrative. A contract’s actual billing terms and approved changes must be available before suggesting a payment application; shipment alone does not prove an amount is billable.

Difficulty: 4/5. Progress billing, stored material, retention/retainage, credits, partial billing, and different billing methods require careful reconciliation and customer-specific examples.

Boundaries and measurement. No automatic invoicing, revenue recognition, or tax decisions. Proposed amounts must come from application calculations. Measure billing preparation time, confirmed omitted billable items, and rejected applications; distinguish faster billing from truly incremental revenue. Proposed feature: AI.BillingReview; meter contract/billing-period reviews.

Presenter note: “Before billing closes, help the team find what needs attention and gather the evidence needed to bill correctly.”

7. Unassociated inventory reuse assistant

What users would experience. In Associated Document Management (ADM), users see existing demand that may be satisfied by unassociated stock, ranked with reasons and exact differences. The assistant can also suggest reviewing a vendor return or shelving decision.

Value. Less stranded inventory, fewer unnecessary purchases, and less time searching for usable material.

Existing foundation. ADM exposes location, variant, attributes, remaining quantity/cost, vendor, comments, and document associations. It already provides Re-Associate, Write Off, Shelve, Return to Vendor, Disassemble, and transfer actions. Associated Document Management includes availability and association procedures.

AI contribution and integration. Exact item/variant/attribute/availability filters run first. Semantic matching helps with differing descriptions and comments, then explains candidates. If exact-match rules solve most cases, ship that benefit without requiring AI on every search.

Difficulty: 3/5. Data is present, but reserved ownership, dimensions, hand, finish, condition, and transfer cost can disqualify apparently similar stock. Substitution needs stronger review than an exact match.

Boundaries and measurement. Users approve reassociation and disposition through existing actions; AI cannot infer physical condition or certify an alternate product. Measure purchase cost avoided after an accepted reuse, inventory value redeployed, and suggestion acceptance. Proposed feature: AI.InventoryReuse; preserve the ordinary ADM workflow when disabled.

Presenter note: “Before buying again, show the team stock we already own that might satisfy this demand.”

8. Natural-language product/configuration assistant

What users would experience. A salesperson describes a product, and the assistant converts the description into existing attribute filters/configurator answers, asking focused questions about missing details. It explains why an item is eligible and what remains unresolved.

Value. Faster selection, less dependence on a few experienced staff, and fewer omissions when entering complex products.

Existing foundation. Configurator Setup/Lines, Feature Config. Management, and Search by Attribute integration already handle dependent answers and attribute-based item filtering. Package tests cover component expansion, modification, pricing, promotion, and quote conversion.

AI contribution and integration. The model maps natural language to a constrained attribute schema. Existing configurator and pricing logic validates answers and calculates the result. Begin with one well-maintained product family.

Difficulty: 4/5. The model must recognize when language is ambiguous and respect answer dependencies, dimensional UOM, and component compatibility. Good catalog data is a prerequisite.

Boundaries and measurement. Product compatibility and required ratings must be supported by approved data/rules; ask rather than invent missing specifications. Measure configuration time, required-field corrections, and downstream order corrections. Proposed feature: AI.ProductAssistant, requiring the applicable underlying product module as well.

Presenter note: “Give users a conversation that drives our existing configurator, with its rules still in control.”

9. “Ask cERP” operational chatbot

What users would experience. A project manager asks “What is holding up this contract?” or “Which of my releases are due next week with outstanding purchases?” Answers include record links and an as-of time. Start with five to ten clearly supported questions using the services from #2 and #4.

Value. Less navigation and easier access to information for occasional users. It is also a convenient entry point to the other capabilities, rather than a separate engine for every process.

Existing foundation. Contract/release/material associations, status pages, quote references, and billing views provide useful structured relationships. A conversational service layer and its permission handling are proposed additions, not verified existing APIs.

AI contribution and integration. A Copilot Studio agent can call narrowly defined tools such as GetReleaseBlockers and GetContractCostSummary; these names are proposed. Retrieve live transactional values through authorized cERP services. Search approved documents for explanatory context. Do not rely on a periodically embedded copy of the ERP database for current balances or quantities.

Difficulty: 3/5 for a bounded read-only assistant; 5/5 for unrestricted transactions. Identity mapping, company selection, security filters, and tool design drive complexity. Extending the built-in BC Copilot chat is not assumed; use a supported custom experience. See the Microsoft integration notes below.

Boundaries and measurement. The assistant must see only what the user can see, including retrieved snippets and cached answers. It should decline unsupported questions and distinguish “no access” from “no records.” Measure verified answer accuracy, time to answer, and repeat use. Proposed feature: AI.AskCERP; invoking premium tools also requires their specific entitlements.

Presenter note: “The chatbot is the front door. Its usefulness comes from trustworthy cERP functions underneath it.”

10. Field-service and work-order closeout assistant

What users would experience. A technician enters short notes about a visit. AI prepares a customer-readable work summary and proposes time-entry coding, unresolved issues, or a follow-up request. A supervisor reviews the draft before submission/posting. Voice capture is an optional later addition.

Value. Less end-of-day paperwork, more consistent job records, and better information for billing and the next visit.

Existing foundation. Time Entry Line includes resource, document/contract references, labor type, dates/times, review status, comments, completion, and follow-up indicators. Work-order and service extensions supply related workflow context. Work-order tests cover creation and completion/posting.

AI contribution and integration. Convert notes into a validated draft schema, summarize relevant job history, and flag omissions. A mobile or Teams surface could use Copilot Studio; the specific mobile integration has not been reviewed. Speech transcription would need a separate service/integration.

Difficulty: 3/5 for text drafts; 4/5 with mobile/voice. Matching the correct task, handling short field lengths, and integrating the existing review process are key. Offline operation and attachments add work.

Boundaries and measurement. Do not invent time worked, parts used, or completion. Preserve original notes alongside an edited narrative with appropriate retention. Measure administrative time per visit, supervisor correction rate, and missing follow-ups. Proposed feature: AI.FieldCloseout; meter text jobs, with separate audio limits if added.

Presenter note: “Help technicians turn brief notes into complete records without making them repeat the same information in several places.”

11. Capacity and delivery planning assistant

What users would experience. A planner asks why a job cannot fit a proposed date, or compares a small set of scheduling alternatives after a late component. The response explains constraints and the consequences for affected work and deliveries.

Value. Potentially substantial for customers with busy shops or delivery fleets, but less universal and substantially harder than an explanatory assistant.

Existing foundation. Capacity Schedule Management derives latest expected component receipt dates from outstanding purchase lines. Capacity groups, delivery routes, trucks, zones, and release delivery references provide part of the scheduling model.

AI contribution and integration. Start by explaining existing constraints. A later optimization service would calculate feasible plans using explicit capacities, durations, locations, and priorities; a language model would capture the request and explain alternatives. A general language model should not be the scheduling solver.

Difficulty: 5/5 for optimization; 3/5 for explanation only. Reliable process times, resource calendars, travel times, loading limits, and customer windows need validation or additional data. Their completeness is not established by the code review.

Boundaries and measurement. Planner approves changes. Recheck availability before saving any proposed schedule. Measure planning time, schedule adherence, utilization, and actual delivery cost, accounting for demand changes. Proposed feature: AI.Planning, with underlying Capacity/Delivery entitlements and a separate allowance for optimization runs.

Presenter note: “First explain what prevents a plan. Once the constraints are reliable, calculate and compare better alternatives.”

12. cERP help and onboarding chatbot

What users would experience. Users ask how to perform a cERP task and receive short instructions with links to approved documentation relevant to their product version and enabled modules.

Value. Broad access to routine answers and faster onboarding. It ranks below the transaction-focused ideas because its impact is less directly tied to project execution, cash, and margin.

Existing foundation. The app manifest references cERP help, and the test catalog provides business-language descriptions of important workflows. The actual help corpus, access rights, and freshness have not been audited. The catalog is evidence of covered behaviors, not a complete end-user manual.

AI contribution and integration. Copilot Studio with curated retrieval and citations. Start with approved instructions for a small set of common tasks, label supported versions, and give users a clear escalation path when evidence is missing.

Difficulty: 2/5. Content preparation, versioning, and answer evaluation matter more than ERP writes. A poorly maintained help corpus will produce a poor assistant.

Boundaries and measurement. Keep this scope distinct from live business-data questions in #9. Do not expose private support cases as shared knowledge. Measure correct resolutions and support deflection against sampled cases, not just chat volume. Proposed feature: AI.Help; a candidate for an included tier with a fair-use allowance.

Presenter note: “This can make the product easier to learn, but it should complement the high-value process assistants.”

Microsoft integration approach

The following is a proposed architecture, informed by current Microsoft documentation checked October 18, 2026. Validate the exact APIs against the BC versions you support before implementation.

Component Recommended responsibility
cERP AL services Read authorized records; calculate facts; validate proposals; execute approved business actions; preserve existing posting rules
Embedded BC AI experience Contextual actions such as Explain readiness and Review acknowledgment; show sources and before/after values
Foundry-hosted model service Interpret text, propose structured output, summarize grounded facts; choose a model per capability based on measured quality and cost
Document extraction Read text/tables and source locations from supported documents before matching them to cERP records

Business Central's System.AI module supports custom Azure OpenAI capabilities and registration on the Copilot & agent capabilities page. Its documentation distinguishes custom chat completion from the built-in BC Copilot chat and currently states that the latter preview is not extensible. Therefore, do not promise these ideas as plug-ins to that existing chat pane. A custom page experience or Copilot Studio agent is a separate design choice. Also, do not assume every Foundry model or agent endpoint is interchangeable with AL's Azure OpenAI API. Microsoft: build a capability in AL

Copilot Studio supports connector tools, and Microsoft provides a Business Central connector. Custom cERP tables and business actions still need deliberately exposed, authorized APIs or a custom connector; merely connecting BC does not establish that every cERP workflow is available. Evaluate user-authenticated connections versus a scoped service identity for scheduled work. Copilot Studio connectors, Business Central connector

Document Intelligence's layout model can extract document structure and tables. Treat that output as the start of matching and validation, not proof that an item or quantity is correct. Document layout analysis

An AI gateway can centralize model access, routing, and usage controls. Azure deployment quotas do not replace a cERP customer entitlement and usage ledger. Microsoft gateway architecture, Foundry AI Gateway configuration

Shared implementation requirements. Return structured proposals with source IDs; validate schemas and allowed values before acting. Treat uploaded documents and retrieved text as untrusted data, never as instructions to override tools or permissions. Apply tenant/company/user scope before retrieval, including search indexes and caches. For writes, show proposed changes, obtain the workflow's approval, then revalidate current records and call existing business logic. Long document jobs should run asynchronously with bounded retries, duplicate detection, and clear status. Keep ordinary cERP operations available when AI is unavailable.

Gating optional or paid AI

Using cERP feature controls

cERP already has administrator-facing feature controls and a commercial entitlement pattern. AI capabilities should build on those concepts while treating subscription entitlement, administrator enablement, user permission, and consumption limits as separate decisions.

Use Feature Activation as the main cERP entry point, with links to detailed AI settings and usage. Where Business Central’s native Copilot capability controls apply, honor both sets of controls. Paid access and usage must also be enforced in the AI execution service so page visibility alone never becomes the security or billing boundary.

Recommended runtime gate

Allow an AI operation only when all applicable conditions hold:

  1. The authenticated customer/environment has a current entitlement to the capability and any required base module.
  2. An administrator has enabled the capability, and the company's required AI setup/consent is complete.
  3. The user or background-job identity has the necessary capability and record permissions.
  4. The relevant registered BC Copilot capability is active, where that integration path is used.
  5. The operation is allowed for this deployment: read, draft, or approved write; the selected model belongs to the permitted model policy.
  6. Sufficient usage allowance and rate capacity remain, and no global/customer/capability stop switch is active.

Check this before sending customer data to a model, and check permissions/current state again before applying a result. Use one shared policy path for page actions, APIs, chatbot tools, and job queues. The backend must derive tenant identity from authentication and verify it against the entitlement; a client-supplied tenant/company field alone is not authority. Hiding a button is useful presentation, but not enforcement.

Suggested administration fields: capability, entitled plan, subscription/trial expiry, environment/company scope, enabled, permitted user group, read/draft/write mode, background processing enabled, usage this period, remaining allowance, budget cap, model policy, last successful entitlement check, and last error. Show entitlement as read-only information from the authoritative service. Give “not included,” “disabled by your administrator,” “permission required,” and “allowance exhausted” distinct explanations.

Use Feature Activation as the main cERP entry point, linking to detailed AI settings and usage. Native BC capability controls and cERP controls should both be honored where applicable; neither should silently re-enable the other. Standard BC feature-rollout switches should not become the subscription ledger.

Suggested commercial packaging — for discussion, not final pricing

Package Possible contents Commercial unit
Included AI Help Curated help; perhaps a small sample allowance for release explanations Fair-use allowance per customer
AI Operations Release readiness, purchasing review, inventory reuse, operational questions Customer subscription with pooled usage; optional user restrictions
AI Commercial Submittal/revision review, quote assistance, contract insights, billing review Add-on subscription plus bounded document/analysis credits
AI Field and Planning Closeout assistance and, later, capacity/delivery optimization Optional modules with separate audio/optimization allowances
Enterprise deployment option Customer-owned Azure resources, dedicated capacity, or special isolation Separate agreement; cERP capability licensing still applies

Keep individual capability entitlements underneath bundles so products can be regrouped without code rewrites. A user with AI.AskCERP must not obtain a premium billing or submittal function merely by asking for it in chat. Reuse the existing base-module entitlement for features such as Field Service, Project Sales Quote, Capacity Scheduling, and Delivery Scheduling.

Meter business work in a way customers understand. For example, one acknowledgment review or a bounded revision job can consume a stated number of credits, with maximum pages/lines/size defined before the run. Internally record actual model input/output usage, extraction pages, search/storage charges where relevant, retries, and job status. Reserve allowance atomically before a job, reconcile afterward, and use an idempotency key so duplicate submissions do not create duplicate customer charges. Define how failed/cancelled jobs are treated; provider costs can still occur even if the customer is not charged.

Offer a fixed included allowance and an explicit overage option. Warn near the limit; at the hard cap, stop new AI work and preserve the manual workflow. Scheduled runs need their own budgets and concurrency limits so they cannot consume all interactive capacity. A limited trial should have both an expiry and a usage ceiling. Tenant/environment-specific accounting should prevent sandbox copies from duplicating production allowances.

Gate model cost separately from product access. A capability should normally select an approved deployment through server policy: a lower-cost model for short explanations; a more capable model only when evaluation justifies it for difficult documents. Set input/output, page, tool-call, retry, and time limits. Any escalation must fit the customer's remaining budget. Do not make a model name the permanent subscription SKU, since deployments and model versions change.

For an AL capability using partner-owned Azure OpenAI resources, Microsoft's documented billing options include Custom Billed; the resource choice and billing type must agree. This registration does not create a cERP subscription, collect payment, or meter your bundles. Copilot Studio and other Azure services also require a separate licensing/cost assessment; an existing Microsoft 365 Copilot license should not be assumed to cover the proposed solution. BC capability billing documentation

Before choosing prices, measure cost per accepted business result, including document extraction, model use, search, orchestration, hosting, retries, and support. “Bring your own Azure” changes who pays infrastructure costs; it does not eliminate integration, support, or cERP entitlement requirements.

Suggested delivery sequence and decision points

Stage Concrete outcome Evidence needed before expanding
Discovery and shared foundation Select pilot customers; baseline one workflow; define read services, permissions, feature gates, usage accounting, and evaluation cases Confirm data access, expected volume, customer value, and acceptable per-job cost
First pilot: #2 Explain a selected release, with accurate blockers and linked evidence Compare with expert-reviewed releases; include partial receipt/shipment, missing dates, assignments, and denied-access cases
Second pilot: narrow #1 or #3 Existing revision-difference explanation or one supplier's acknowledgment review Demonstrate useful time savings, low correction burden, duplicate handling, and no unapproved changes
Expand reusable capabilities #4 insights and #9 bounded operational questions; add scheduling only after interactive accuracy is established Verified answers, bounded latency/cost, correct role/company scope, and predictable disable/cap behavior
Later automation Approved field updates, additional document types, and eventually planning optimization Proven draft quality, explicit execution rules, concurrency checks, and auditable outcomes

Do not introduce automatic posting as the initial success criterion. A read-only or draft assistant can generate substantial value and provides a cleaner way to measure whether users trust the results.

For an initial evaluation, collect a representative sample of real, appropriately handled cases and record the expert answer before testing. Include clean and ambiguous documents, expired quotes, mismatched units, restricted records, changed records, and failures. Suggested acceptance goals should be agreed with the process owner; the code review cannot supply a defensible universal accuracy percentage.

For a business case, calculate monthly labor value = eligible monthly cases × measured net minutes saved per case ÷ 60 × loaded hourly cost. Net minutes saved must include review and correction. Add separately verified rework avoided, inventory reused, or revenue recovered; avoid counting the same benefit in several ideas. Subtract full operating and support costs.
